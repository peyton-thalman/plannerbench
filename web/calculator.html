Finding 3 · With a calculator

Give it a calculator it can trust, and some models get it right. Not all.

The plain answer

I turned my answer key into a calculator an AI model can use while it answers. It helps a lot, but only if the calculator shows its work and the model actually uses it well. It rescued some models, including a cheap one, and did nothing for others.

One example: it argued with the calculator

Claude Haiku 5.5 asked the calculator for a couple’s tax, got the right number, and replied:

—

It used its own wrong reading of a new 2025 tax break for people over 65 instead, and got the tax wrong. When the calculator also showed the rule and the IRS form behind the number, it stopped arguing.

What I mean by a “calculator”

In AI, a “tool” is a program a model can call in the middle of an answer, the way you’d reach for a calculator. Mine is the planning engine: code for 2026 federal tax, Social Security, required withdrawals from retirement accounts and Medicare premiums, checked against IRS, Social Security and Medicare publications. The model still has to decide what to type in and how to put the pieces together, which is where it can still go wrong.

Three calculator tests on this page

  1. Calculator test 1: number vs rule (October). One model, the hardest first-test questions. A right number wasn’t enough; the rule behind it was.
  2. Calculator test 2: who it rescues (November). Eight models on the newer, harder set. A cheap model jumped; three others didn’t move.
  3. Calculator test 3: whole-household decisions. Real choices over 25 years, three models. Plus ChatGPT without a calculator, and a short test: leave a fact out, and see if it asks.

    Right parts, wrong assembly

    GPT-6.1 Sol took the federal numbers from the calculator for an Arizona tax question, then did the state part itself:

    “Arizona’s standard deduction does not include the federal age-65 addition… $17,900 × 2.5% = $447.50. ANSWER: 448”

    It missed Arizona’s separate $2,100 exemption for people 65 and older. The right answer is $395. The calculator gave it correct parts; putting them together was its own work.

          For the technical reader

          The calculator is an MCP server (the Model Context Protocol, the standard way to plug tools into AI models). The benchmark runners and the chat add-on call the same code. The first test offered federal tax, taxable Social Security, required withdrawals, Social Security benefits, Medicare premiums and tax tables. The November set offered those plus a year-by-year scenario runner; the Roth-conversion solver and the chat front door were left out to keep the instructions short.
