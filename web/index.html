AI gets simple retirement questions right. On real ones, it depends which AI you ask.

Trust the best AIs on a single rule. Check them on your whole situation, and check what you type in.

The bottom line

  • One rule (a question that needs just one): the best AIs were almost always right. Claude Sonnet 5.5 got — of questions like this year’s tax on an income.
  • Several rules at once (taxes, Social Security and Medicare affecting each other): it depends which AI. GPT-6.1 Sol got — of my hardest questions. ChatGPT’s model on my regular subscription got — (a different model and setup, tested separately).
  • Some accept impossible facts, so check what you type. Even the best two missed — of the mistakes I planted (like a retiree who is 14); ChatGPT missed —.

How sure am I? Separate programs that didn’t write each other must agree on every answer. No CPA (licensed tax accountant) has signed off yet; that’s next. How I checked →

What to do this week

Ask your AI one retirement question you care about. Check every age, balance and income it used, and ask which rule it applied. If a fact is wrong or it can’t name the rule, don’t act on the number; take anything you can’t undo, like when to claim Social Security, to a professional.

The story in 60 seconds

The problem. I’m a door-to-door financial advisor. More and more clients ask ChatGPT about retirement, and nobody checks the answers.

What I built. An answer key first (the right answer to every question), from a planning engine (a program for the 2026 IRS, Social Security and Medicare rules) that separate programs recheck. Then the questions. AI agents built it; I made the calls.

The strongest result. Handed the right answer by my calculator, a model argued with it. Once the calculator also showed the rule and the IRS form line, it agreed: — → — → — on — hard questions; — on — held back.

The limits. Small samples, an AI judge on one test, no CPA sign-off yet.

What I decided

  • Build the checker before the questions. A second engine, written separately, matches mine on — random households.
  • Fix the calculator’s reply, not the prompt. Show the rule, not just the number.
  • Keep a test set nobody tunes on. Secret questions, only for checking that a fix is real.
  • Have another company’s model audit it. GPT-6.1 Sol found — errors; all fixed and published.
