AI gets simple retirement questions right. On real ones, it depends which AI you ask.

On the one-rule questions I tested, the two Claude models were right about — of the time. On questions where several rules meet, the scores depended heavily on which AI. In every test, some models accepted facts that couldn’t be true.

The bottom line

One rule = one tax or retirement rule, like this year’s tax brackets. Several rules at once = taxes, Social Security and Medicare affecting each other, the way they do in a real household.

  • One rule: Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Haiku 5.5 were almost always right. Sonnet got — of questions like this year’s tax on an income (first test, October).
  • Several rules at once: it depended which AI. On my hardest questions, GPT-6.1 Sol got —, GPT-6.1 Terra — and Claude Sonnet 5.5 —. GPT-6 Luna through Codex (the model on my ChatGPT subscription) got —, in a different setup, tested separately.
  • Some accepted impossible facts, so check what you type. Of the mistakes I planted (like a retiree who is 14), GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Sonnet 5.5, the two best at catching them without false alarms, still missed —; GPT-6 Luna missed —.

How sure am I? Outside tax software built by other people (PolicyEngine-US) rechecks the first test’s tax answers; every other answer rests on two separately written programs agreeing; and two checks by a person are still to come: a CPA’s (licensed tax accountant’s) sign-off and a spot-check of the AI judge. About ChatGPT. The regular ChatGPT app uses GPT-6 Luna, a different, cheaper model than GPT-6.1 Sol (the — one). I ran Luna through OpenAI’s Codex app with code and web turned off, in small batches, so it’s close to, but not the same as, typing into the ChatGPT window.

What to do this week

Ask your AI one retirement question you care about. Check every age, balance and income it used, and ask which rule it applied. If a fact is wrong or it can’t name the rule, don’t act on the number; take anything you can’t undo, like when to claim Social Security, to a professional.

The story in 60 seconds

The problem. I’m a door-to-door financial advisor. More and more clients ask ChatGPT about retirement, and nobody checks the answers.

What I built. An answer key first (the right answer to every question), from a planning engine (a program I had built for the 2026 IRS, Social Security and Medicare rules) that separate programs recheck. Then the questions. AI agents built it; I made the calls.

The strongest result. Handed the right answer by my calculator, a model argued with it. Once the calculator also showed the rule and the IRS form line, it agreed: — → — → — on — hard questions, and — on — held-back questions (secret ones never used while building, kept only to check that a fix is real).

The limits. Small samples; on the wrong-facts test, an AI judge (another AI model reading each reply) decided whether a model named the mistake; no CPA sign-off yet.

What I decided

  • Build the checker before the questions. A second program, written without seeing the first, agreed on all — test households.
  • Fix the calculator’s reply, not the prompt. Show the rule, not just the number.
  • Keep secret test questions. Held-back questions are never used while building; they only check that a fix is real.
  • Have another company’s model audit it. GPT-6.1 Sol found — errors; all fixed and published.
