AI gets simple retirement questions right. On real ones, it depends which AI you ask.

The bottom line

  • One rule at a time: the best AIs I tested were almost always right. Ask this year’s tax on an income, or how much you must take out of an IRA (a retirement account you haven’t paid tax on yet), and Claude Sonnet 5.5 got — right.

  • AI can accept impossible facts, so check what you type. Given client files with one planted mistake each (like a retiree who is 14), even the best models didn’t catch — of them, and ChatGPT didn’t catch —.

What to do this week

Ask the AI you use one retirement question you care about. Then read back every age, balance and income in its answer, and ask it to name the rule it used and where that rule comes from (an IRS form, Social Security’s rules). If a fact is wrong or it can’t name the rule, don’t act on the number. Take anything you can’t undo, like when to claim Social Security, to a professional.

Why I made this

I’m a financial advisor, and I meet most people by knocking on their door. My dad became a financial advisor in 1999; watching him take care of people is why I do this work.

More and more people tell me they get financial advice from ChatGPT. When I ask how they know it’s right, they Google it, and the top result is often another AI’s answer. So I wrote the questions planners work through every week, worked out the right answer to each one, and tested the AI models people use.

The planning engine
The program that works out the answer key from the 2026 IRS, Social Security and Medicare rules. I had AI agents build it; programs that didn’t write it check it. Turned into a tool an AI can call while it answers, it’s “the calculator.”
