AI on its own · findings 1 and 2

AI gets one rule right. When rules meet, it depends which AI you ask.

The plain answer

Ask about one well-known rule and the best AIs I tested are almost always right. Ask a question where several rules meet, the way they do in a real household, and scores spread out: in my newer, harder set, the best model got — right and — cheap models scored under 30%. In the first test, many models made the same mistake on a tax rule that is new since 2025.

    What the results mean, and what they don’t

    • The math isn’t the problem. In the first test, models handled long-standing rules well. They slipped on the newest rules and on several rules meeting at once.
    • It isn’t one company’s blind spot. In the first test, DeepSeek and GLM made the same senior-deduction misreading as Claude. Models know the rules as they were, not as they are.
    • Price per word misleads. A model that’s cheap to run can cost more per right answer if it thinks so long it never answers. The cost columns are in the full tables above.
    • It’s still small. —–— questions per task per model in the first test, — in the newer set. A gap of a few points is within the noise; the ranges show how much.
