Results · AI on its own

AI can do the math. It often doesn’t know this year’s rule.

In short

Ask about one old, well-known rule and AI is almost always right. Ask a question where several rules meet, the way they do in a real household, and it gets most of them wrong. Most of the wrong answers come from one rule that changed in 2025. Show the AI that rule and it gets the question right.

    How these were run

    Claude models were asked 10 questions per task through Anthropic’s API; the others 15 per task through OpenRouter, each pinned to one named host with data collection off. “Open weights” means anyone can download the model; fp8 and bf16 say how compressed the host’s copy is. “Never finished” means the model spent its whole 32,000-token budget thinking and never answered; that counts as not right. When two bands overlap heavily, the gap between those models may be noise.

    What the mistakes would cost a client

    A right-or-wrong score treats a Medicare premium that is ten cents off the same as a required withdrawal that is $9,000 too high. A planner wouldn’t. So here each answer is scored by how many dollars a year it is off. A miss of $1,000 a year or more counts as a big one.

    Reading the results

    What they mean, and what they don’t

    • The math isn’t the problem. Models handle long-standing rules well. They slip on the newest ones and on several rules interacting at once.
    • It isn’t one lab’s blind spot. DeepSeek and GLM made the same senior-deduction misreading as Claude. gpt-oss-120b used the RMD table that was replaced in 2022 on every RMD it got wrong. Models know the rules as they were, not as they are.
    • Price per token misleads. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash tied Claude Haiku 5.5 overall, and Haiku was still the cheapest per right answer.
    • The compliance questions are too easy. They define each issue in the question, so every model scores 91–100%. The next version names issues without defining them.
    • It’s still small. 10–15 questions per task per model. A gap of a few points is within the noise; the intervals show how much.
