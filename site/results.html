Results · models alone

Models do the math. They don’t reliably know the current rule.

In short

Models are near-perfect when one long-standing rule applies, and fall apart when several rules meet in one household. Most of the misses trace back to one rule that changed in 2025. Give a model that rule and it gets the question right.

    How these were run

    Claude models were asked 10 questions per task through Anthropic’s API; the others 15 per task through OpenRouter, each pinned to one named host with data collection off. “Open weights” means anyone can download the model; fp8 and bf16 say how compressed the host’s copy is. “Ran out of tokens” means the model spent its whole 32,000-token budget thinking and never answered; that counts as not right. When two bands overlap heavily, the gap between those models may be noise.

    What the mistakes would cost a client

    Accuracy counts a Medicare premium ten cents off the same as a required distribution $9,000 too high. A planner wouldn’t. Here each answer is scored by how many dollars a year it is off; a miss of $1,000 a year or more is called material.

    Reading the results

    What they mean, and what they don’t

    • The math isn’t the problem. Models handle long-standing rules well. They slip on the newest ones and on several rules interacting at once.
    • It isn’t one lab’s blind spot. DeepSeek and GLM made the same senior-deduction misreading as Claude. gpt-oss-120b used the RMD table that was replaced in 2022 on every RMD it got wrong. Models know the rules as they were, not as they are.
    • Price per token misleads. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash tied Claude Haiku 5.5 overall, and Haiku was still the cheapest per right answer.
    • The compliance questions are too easy. They define each issue in the question, so every model scores 91–100%. The next version names issues without defining them.
    • It’s still small. 10–15 questions per task per model. A gap of a few points is within the noise; the intervals show how much.
