With the engine

Hand it the right answer, and it argues. Show it the rule, and it listens.

In short

The planning engine is also a set of tools any model can call while it answers, over MCP (Model Context Protocol, the standard way to plug tools into AI models). A tool that only returned the right number got overruled. A tool that showed the rule and cited the IRS form line fixed every hard question. On whole-household decisions, both models tested got every decision and every number right with the engine.

Step one · the benchmark’s hardest questions

The same 50 questions, three ways

These are the 50 hardest of the benchmark’s questions (multi-step and stacked), so Claude Haiku 5.5 scores lower here alone than its 77% across all of them. Tools 1.0 sent back only the answer; tools 1.1 sends back the answer, the rule behind it and the IRS form line. The tools are general calculators (federal tax, taxable Social Security, RMDs, claiming age, Medicare premiums, tax tables), not one per question, so the model still has to pick the inputs and combine the results.

    Step two · the Gauntlet

    Real retirement decisions, alone and with the engine

    The Gauntlet has 110 questions in 10 families: 77 public and 33 held back to check results later. The first run below asked one from each family plus a trap. Each question is a whole household (about 16 facts: Social Security, IRAs, a pension, a brokerage account) and a choice an advisor makes for real: whether and how much to convert to Roth, when to claim, how to give, how to empty an inherited IRA. Getting it right means stacking at least three rules across 25 years.
